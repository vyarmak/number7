**Proceed to paper, with the gap filter flagged and the drawdown result understood.** Four of
five gates pass. The monkey test fails on its drawdown leg, and that failure is informative
rather than disqualifying — see below.

|---|---|---|
| Walk-forward efficiency | 1.127 over 17 windows (floor 0.5) | **pass** |
| Deflated Sharpe | 0.9952 vs hurdle 0.98 | **pass**, but weak by construction |
| Gate 6 (equal-weight sniff test) | Sharpe ratio 1.09 | not suspicious |

The DSR pass is weak *by design*, not by accident: a one-point replication has no search, so
`expected_max_sr(1, ·) = 0` and DSR degenerates to PSR against a zero benchmark. Spec §10
anticipated this — it is why the reference replication is registered separately from any
searched variant. It should not be read as evidence of edge.

## The monkey failure

This is the most informative result in the run, and it only became visible because the null is
matched. The monkeys are `ClenowMomentum(shuffle_seed=·)` — identical eligibility filters,
regime gate, ATR sizing and book resolution, with **only the ranking permuted**. Average gross
exposure: candidate 0.782, monkeys 0.729.

- **Profit percentile 1.000** — the strategy out-earned *every one* of 1,000 rank-shuffled
  nulls. Momentum ranking carries real return information.
- **Drawdown percentile 0.001** — it had a deeper drawdown than 99.9% of them.

`beats_90` requires profit ≥ 0.90 **and** drawdown ≥ 0.50, so the gate fails on the second leg.

The interpretation is not "the strategy is noise" — a 1.000 profit percentile is the opposite
of that. It is that **ranking concentrates the book into names that fall together.** Random
rankings spread across the eligible set and diversify; momentum ranking selects a correlated
cohort. That is precisely the common-factor exposure ATR parity does not address, and it is
the same quantitative argument that makes the deferred portfolio risk layer a prerequisite for
capital: 25 positions each targeting a 10bp daily move imply ~8% annualized volatility if
independent, but ~23% at 0.30 average pairwise correlation.

Against the *old* unmatched null (`RandomTopN`, always fully invested, equal-weight) this
comparison would have measured exposure profiles rather than ranking skill and would not have
surfaced. The gauntlet fix earned its place on its first real run.

## Regime gate

53 episodes, 1,178 off-sessions = **20.7% of history spent not admitting new names**.

The headline 27/53 = 50.9% hit rate is misleading, because episodes are wildly unequal. 38% of
them are ≤5 sessions — noise around the SMA, together only 59 sessions. Weighted by how long
the gate actually held us out:

| measure | value |
|---|---|
| unweighted hit rate | 50.9% |
| **session-weighted hit rate** | **72.2%** |
| median episode | 7 sessions |
| longest three | 259 / 162 / 98 sessions = 44% of all off-time, **all hits** |
| cumulative index decline avoided across hits | −58.8% |
| cumulative gain foregone on false positives | +21.3% |

The three long episodes are 2008-05-20→2009-05-29 (−33.07% through), 2022-04-08→2022-11-29
(−10.95%), and 2007-12-27→2008-05-16 (−2.91%). So the gate is a coin flip at short horizons
and reliable at the horizons that matter — it is right when being right is expensive. Spec
§11.2 worried about resting the gate on N=1; there are three substantial realizations here,
not one, though two of them belong to the same 2007–09 episode cluster.

**Paired ablation (the discriminating test):** disabling the gate raises average gross from
0.78 to 0.91 and CAGR from 9.05% to 9.77%, but *lowers* return per unit of exposure from
11.57% to 10.75% and deepens max drawdown from −27.8% to −39.4%. Gated − ungated is
**+0.82pp on exposure-adjusted return and +11.6pp on drawdown**. The gate earns its place.

**No whipsaw at transitions.** Turnover at regime transitions is 27.92% against a 27.98%
average over 150 transition-adjacent rebalances — statistically indistinguishable. The known
SMA-crossing failure mode does not appear on this data. Worth re-checking if the rebalance
cadence ever changes, since weekly rebalancing already smooths crossing effects.

Three of four components earn their place. R² as a smoothness penalty adds 0.48pp of
exposure-adjusted return. ATR parity gives up raw return but buys 8.6pp of drawdown and 0.03
of Sharpe — the trade the design intends.

**The gap filter does not.** Removing it improves every axis simultaneously: Sharpe
0.55 → 0.60, exposure-adjusted return 11.57% → 12.60%, drawdown −27.8% → −25.2%. That is not a
marginal call.

### Why — and why we are not changing it

Investigated rather than assumed, since spec §6.2 records that the source alternates between
"gap" and "single daily move" and we chose the overnight reading:

- The overnight definition `|open/prev_close − 1| > 15%` fires on **1,512 constituent-days**
  out of 2.85M (0.025%). The "single daily move" alternative `|close/prev_close − 1|` would
  fire on 4,972 (0.083%) — **3.3× more**. Our chosen definition is the *more conservative* of
  the two readings, so this is not a case of having picked the aggressive one.
- Norgate's open is the first print from any venue while the close is the listing-exchange
  auction, so vendor artifacts were plausible. They are rare: only **4.7%** of overnight trips
  are open spikes that closed back within 5%, and 70.4% coincide with a >15% close-to-close
  move — i.e. the filter is mostly catching genuine large moves, not print noise.
- The cost is not the trip count but the **90-session disqualification** each trip triggers.
  That blocks **3.50% of constituent-days on average — but 8.1% in 2008, 8.4% in 2009 and
  9.7% in 2020.** The filter removes candidates disproportionately during exactly the
  high-volatility periods when the regime gate is already cutting exposure, compounding
  de-risking when the book is already de-risked.

So the mechanism is understood, and it is a property of the rule rather than of our
implementation of it. **We are not changing it.** Per the anti-fitting clause (§11.4), altering
a published parameter because our data prefers the alternative is exactly the nudging the
clause forbids; it would spawn a new pre-registration and count as a new trial. Recorded here
as a finding for the searched-variant registration to address deliberately, if ever.

## Benchmark comparison

Against the equal-weight index benchmark over the same window: benchmark CAGR 10.91%,
sleeve 9.05% — the sleeve **underperforms on raw return by 1.85pp**. On the terms spec §11.2.3
mandates, it wins: Sharpe 0.552 vs 0.505 (ratio 1.09, below the 2.0 "suspiciously good"
threshold), beta 0.50, and 11.57% return per unit of gross exposure against a fully-invested
benchmark. Raw CAGR versus a fully-invested benchmark through a long bull punishes the design
for working as intended — under-investment is the expected behaviour, not a defect.

## Data-integrity checks

Both open questions from the plan were settled empirically rather than left unknown.

**Delisting returns are embedded — the backtest does not overstate.** Checked against the live
bridge: SIVBQ (SVB Financial, bank failure) runs from a $737.77 peak down to a terminal
**$0.01**, and ATVI (acquired for $95 cash) ends at $94.42. Norgate's total-return series
carries price to its true terminal value rather than stopping at the last healthy quote, so
exiting a delisted name at its last available close is approximately correct. The path is
rarely exercised anyway — 5 delisting exits across 22 years, because the MA filter and rank
remove a collapsing name long before it stops quoting.

**Membership intervals are effective dates, not announcement dates.** TSLA's stint in the
snapshot starts **2020-12-21**, the effective date, against a 2020-11-16 announcement. The
book therefore cannot trade the S&P-addition pop, which would have been a real look-ahead.
Deletion-date exits remain the classic worst-execution point and are a recorded known cost.

## Fidelity assessment

Does the overlap window support fidelity? **Qualitatively yes, quantitatively unverified.**

The system behaves as the published description says it should: it holds ~25 names, sits
around 78% invested, cuts exposure in bear regimes, produces a drawdown roughly half the
index's, and shows a beta near 0.50. WFE above 1.0 across 17 windows says out-of-sample
performance is not degraded relative to in-sample — unusual and worth noting.

What this run does **not** establish is a numerical match to the book's published figures. We
never had the published series to compare against, only its qualitative description, so
"fidelity" here means "the mechanism reproduces the described behaviour", not "the equity curve
matches". Specific known discrepancies, all declared platform adaptations rather than defects:
weekly re-sizing against the book's second-Wednesday cadence; a 2004 start that omits the
dot-com bear; SPY rather than the index itself as the regime proxy; a 0% cash rate.

## Declared platform adaptations

- **Cadence:** the published system maintains the roster weekly but re-sizes every second
  Wednesday; we rebalance weekly (Tuesday default) with MOC fills. Turnover differences trace
  to this, not to a bug.
- **Cash rate:** primary runs earn 0.00% on cash; the 2%/4% diagnostics bound the handicap.
- **Regime proxy:** capital-adjusted SPY. `$SPX` from Norgate's US Indices database is a
  registered variant, not a free swap.
- **Gap definition:** overnight (`|open / prev_close − 1|`), the more conservative of the two
  readings in the source — see the gap-filter section.

## What must happen before capital
